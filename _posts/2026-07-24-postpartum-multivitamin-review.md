## 1. Theralogix TheraNatal Lactation Complete

**Price:** around $38–$88

This is the "I want one thing that covers everything" option.

It's NSF certified, which matters more than most brands let on, because NSF testing verifies that what's on the label is actually in the bottle. That's not a small thing in a supplement industry with famously loose oversight.

**What stands out:**

- High-dose vitamin D3, marketed as enough to potentially replace separate infant vitamin D drops for exclusively breastfed babies. The [American Academy of Pediatrics](https://publications.aap.org/aapbooks/monograph/794/chapter-abstract/16750729/Vitamin-D-Iron-and-Zinc-Supplementation-Baby-Early?redirectedFrom=fulltext) does recommend infant vitamin D supplementation unless maternal intake is high enough, so this claim has real backing.
- Covers iron, iodine, folate, choline, calcium, and DHA in one regimen.
- No fishy aftertaste for most users, which matters more than it sounds like when you're already nauseated.

> "I used theralogix prenatals and the lactation set. From what I could tell they are great. 17 month old is super healthy and I was able to nurse for 13 months before he self-weaned." — *Anonymous*

**Where it loses points:**

- Three pills a day (2 tablets, 1 softgel). If pill fatigue is real for you, this adds up fast.
- The 13-week bulk purchase is a big upfront hit if money's tight this month.
- Scattered reports of quality control issues, including moldy tablets. Rare, but worth knowing before you commit.

> "Expensive, getting doen 3 pills per day was kind of a struggle in the first trimester" — *Anonymous*

**Best for you if:**

- You want a single, comprehensive system
- You don't mind the pill count
- And the price doesn't make you wince

## 2. Nature Made Postnatal Multivitamin + DHA

**Price:** $18.60–$24.94

This is the drugstore workhorse.

It's the one your OB's office probably has a sample of in a drawer somewhere, and there's a reason: it's affordable, widely stocked, and covers the core nutrients you actually need postpartum — iron, folate, iodine, vitamin D, calcium, and DHA.

Choline and iodine deserve a beat of attention here:

- [Choline](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Choline-HealthProfessional/) supports your baby's brain development through breast milk, and most American diets fall short of the recommended intake even before a baby enters the picture.
- [Iodine](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/) needs actually go up during lactation, not down, because it's transferred through milk.

**What stands out:**

- One softgel a day. Simple, no juggling.
- Meets the basic checklist most OBs ask for.
- Easy to find at any Target run or pharmacy pickup.

> "I was skeptical about these vitamins at first it seems like it has all the same vitamins and the amounts as prenatals- which they don't! I noticed that my skin is glowing, my nails are growing, and my hair!!! I need to mention that I did not get night sweats with these vitamins." — *Anonymous*

**Where it loses points:**

- The most common complaint by far is a strong fishy or seaweed smell, especially from capsules that have sat in a hot car or a damp bathroom cabinet.
- Some bottles arrive with leaking or broken softgels, which is unpleasant but not dangerous.
- Not non-GMO, and contains a few filler ingredients that "clean label" shoppers will notice.

> "I am currently on my third bottle. My only complaint is my last bottle had a very fishy smell. I thought it was a one off and purchased another thinking this would solve my problem, however I ran into the same issue." — *Anonymous*

**Best for you if:**

- Budget and simplicity matter more than a fragrance-free experience
- And you can store the bottle somewhere cool and dry

> "If you don't mind the fish oil smell when you open the bottle or pop your vitamin in your mouth this is a great postnatal. I keep mine in the fridge now and it helps keep the smell to a minimum." — *Anonymous*

## 3. Naturelo Postnatal Multivitamin for Breastfeeding Moms

**Price:** $39.95

This one is built specifically around the breastfeeding relationship, not just general postpartum recovery.

It includes traditional galactagogues: fenugreek, blessed thistle, fennel, alfalfa, and nettle.

Here's the honest part: the research on galactagogues is mixed, not miraculous. A [Cochrane systematic](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD011505.pub2/full) review found limited, low-quality evidence for herbal supply boosters, and effects vary widely from person to person. That doesn't mean it won't work for you — it means don't expect a switch to flip overnight.

**What stands out:**

- Plant-based, non-GMO, free from soy and gluten.
- No strong aftertaste, and capsules are easy to take.
- Many users report better energy and smoother postpartum recovery, separate from any milk supply effect.

> "It has increased my breast milk production so incredibly that I now have a large supply of frozen breast milk." — *Anonymous*

**Where it loses points:**

- No DHA in the formula, so you'll need it from fatty fish, an algae-based DHA supplement, or your prenatal leftovers.
- Three capsules a day.
- The milk supply promise is the headline, but it's not guaranteed science.

> "It's not that I think these pills are bad but they did not work for me and you cannot return the bottle once opened. These pills decreased my milk supply after only using for one week. I did take them for 3 weeks because I did not think it was the pills. Once I stopped taking them my milk supply went back to normal. The ingredients in these pills will not work for everyone." — *Anonymous*

**Best for you if:**

- You want a clean-label formula
- And you're curious about lactation herbs
- But you're not banking your entire supply on them

## 4. Ritual Essential for Women Postnatal Multivitamin

**Price:** $42.98

If nausea has made every other vitamin a fight, this is the one people bring up first.

The delayed-release capsule design means it starts dissolving further down your digestive tract instead of hitting your stomach right away, which is often why people tolerate it better on an empty stomach.

**What stands out:**

- Small, smooth, easy-to-swallow capsules with a mint or citrus scent tucked inside.
- Vegan, gluten-free, no artificial colors, with genuinely transparent sourcing information on their site.
- Many users report fewer nausea flare-ups compared to bulkier softgels.

> "I can take them on an empty stomach, and that's a huge deal for me, considering I am easily nauseated" — *Anonymous*

**Where it loses points:**

- No calcium in the formula. If your diet is already low in dairy or fortified alternatives, you'll want to close that gap elsewhere.
- The mint flavor is polarizing. Some people love it, some find it too strong first thing in the morning.
- It's the priciest option here, and the subscription model means it's an ongoing cost, not a one-time buy.

> "Not too sure who thought this was a good idea but treated gross. The ridiculously heavy mint flavor is just a plain bad idea" vs. "I loved taking this postpartum! The pills were easy to take and had a minty/eucalyptus flavor/aftertaste."

**Best for you if:**

- Nausea has been your dealbreaker with every other vitamin you've tried
- And you're willing to pay more for that comfort

## Conclusion

There's no single "best" postpartum vitamin, only the one that matches your budget, your stomach, and your actual daily routine.

If pills make you gag, don't force a three-a-day regimen just because it looks comprehensive on paper. If money's tight, the affordable option covering your core nutrients is genuinely enough. Trust what your body is telling you, not what the packaging promises.

## FAQs

### **Do you actually need a postpartum vitamin if you already took a prenatal?**

Yes, in most cases. Nutrient needs, especially for iodine and choline, actually increase during lactation compared to pregnancy. A postnatal formula is built around that shift, not just a continuation of pregnancy needs.

### **Can you just keep taking your prenatal instead of switching?**

You can, and many providers are fine with it short term. But prenatals are formulated for fetal development, not milk production, so you may be missing adequate choline, iodine, or DHA depending on the brand. Ask your OB or midwife at your postpartum visit.

### **Is it safe to combine a postnatal vitamin with separate DHA or calcium?**

Generally yes, as long as you're not stacking multiple high-dose iron or vitamin A sources on top of each other. Check total daily amounts against the label, and loop in your provider if you're taking several supplements at once.

### **Will lactation herbs like fenugreek actually increase your milk supply?**

Maybe, but the evidence is inconsistent and effects vary by person. Cochrane's review found the quality of existing studies too limited to confirm a reliable effect. If supply is a real concern, pair any supplement with support from a lactation consultant.

### **How long should you take a postpartum vitamin?**

Most providers recommend continuing through the full duration of breastfeeding, and often for a few months after weaning while your body replenishes stores like iron and vitamin D. Your OB can help tailor this to your bloodwork.

### **What if you are not breastfeeding at all?**

You still likely need some postpartum support, especially iron if you lost blood during delivery, but your needs will look different than a breastfeeding parent's. A standard postnatal without the lactation-specific herbs or extra DHA may be more appropriate.
